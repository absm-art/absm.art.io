Sahira walked through the stone wreckage of the ancient catacomb, motes of rocky dust drifting in the wake of her footfalls. The ornate walls were lit by glowing altars of gem tucked into the walls and ceilings, detailed carvings in the material depicting mysterious legends and family lineages from an age long gone.

It wasn’t quite stone- some kind of polymer. It had lasted thousands of years and was softly worn like new sandstone, patches of now-faded color betraying the vibrant culture that once thrived here.

A culture Sahira now realized she would never know. A people, a history that was forever beyond her reach. Nothing the monster could show her would be what she wanted- her family.

She climbed into a low, cramped dwelling, past fallen shelves and a battered couch frame made of the same substance, with only fuzzy stains betraying anything that wasn’t made of the alloy. Even the mold had long desiccated and turned to dust. 

She came to the doorway of an antechamber. A massive, softly luminous geode sat embedded in the far wall, casting a blue and purple veil of calm light across the room. She looked down, and the deep brown of her arms shimmered with indigo light. Her freckles shined gold and iridescent in the dark glow of the ancient bedroom.

--

“The catacombs,” the machine’s avatar had said, bright and blue and horrifyingly human. “There is a form of radiation there that is incompatible with the type of processors which my systems use. Other humanoids have not survived the journey.”
  
“You think I will,” replied Sahira. “So why not dissect me, figure out how I tick, clone me. You’ve had me for all this time.”

It said, “I think there is much, much more to this puzzle than simply your genes, child.”

--

She delved deeper into the tomb.

Now she began to find the poor creatures that the monster had enslaved before her, scattered every few hundred meters along the corridor indicated on the map she carried. The shimmering geodes in the ceiling illuminated their mummified forms, digested by the bacteria of their own bodies from within, sterilized and mummified from without by the same ionizing radiation that glistened harmlessly back at her from beneath her skin.

Here and there were scattered bodies of something else, stripped skeletons with heavy set bones and crystalline growths of turquoise and green oxidized copper. They began to fill the rooms and halls along her path. She leaned down to examine the meatless femur of one of the apparent humans, feeling the surface.

It was pitted and scarred with tiny spherical boreholes all across its surface.

--

“There is a creature within the heart of the dead moon. Once upon a time, it was dangerous and terrifying. Now, it is the unthinkable end, waiting to awaken. I believe you can prevent that end.” The glowing child reached out its hand.

--

She emerged through a final low doorway into a broad, empty chamber, with constellations of dancing crystal lights stretching away into the distance. The stonework floor gave way abruptly to a black pool that yawned into eternity.
 
Abject terror shot through Sahira. The chamber from her dreams.

.

.

.

.

.

.

.

She couldn’t do it. This was impossible. It would consume her, it would snuff out all of her light, it would destroy everything she was in an instant. It had destroyed her whole civilization.

She knew what she was here to do. But she was also sure that she would never emerge from that pool again.

.

.

.

.

.

.

.

The void bore no motion, and the room was so silent that Sahira heard the creak of her own hair, the rush of blood in her ears, the groan of her diaphragm.

.

.

.

.

.

.

.

A single ripple cast towards her from somewhere impossibly far off across the void, the only reflection on the surface of this vast ocean. Starry glints of gemstone light played on the surface as the wide arc approached. It broke effortlessly on the beach of the tiled floor.

Instantly, a screaming, crashing wail droned through Sahira’s skull. She thought her eyes were going to explode. She fell to her knees, clutching the sides of her head, gasping for air.

Echoing madness and formless, wordless chaos poured into her mind. Thoughts beyond the mind of living things and visions of shattered realities flashed and burned into her memory.

This thing, this monster, hungered with a fury and rage that defied her reeling mind.

It wanted nothing, and everything. It beckoned to her, stretched its consciousness to her- it would have reached out to consume her in an instant.

.

.

.

.

.

.

.

But it couldn’t. It was restrained, frozen, unable to move or motivate itself.

It was waiting.
